📘 How this model works: Torricelli's Law & 2D Porous Diffusion ▼

1. Torricelli Outflow: Discharge velocity through the 2 mm cap orifice follows \(v = \sqrt{2gh}\), where \(h\) is water head height in the inverted bottle. The volumetric flow rate is given by:

Q = Cd · A · √(2gh) · (1 - Clogging) · F_backpressure · K_calib

Here \(C_d \approx 0.60\). In real soil, porous contact at the cap creates hydraulic backpressure \(F_{back}\) as the root zone saturates, preventing rapid flooding.

2. 2D Capillary Soil Moisture Diffusion: The 20×20 matrix models moisture migration governed by the finite-difference diffusion equation: ∂θ/∂t = D ∇²θ - Evaporation - RootUptake. Coco-peat has a high diffusion coefficient (\(D=0.18\)) and high field capacity (85%), ensuring uniform moisture wicking.

3. Plant Moisture Stress: Each species has an optimal band (e.g. Mint: 45%–75%). Moisture below optimal causes wilting stress; moisture above 80% starves roots of oxygen.

📘 How this model works: Comparative Loss Allocation & The 70% Claim ▼

In conventional can-watering, high surface solar exposure drives 35–45% immediate evaporation. In addition, rapid pouring overflows small bottle planter rims (20% runoff) and causes gravity percolation (15%) past shallow roots. Thus, over 70% of applied water never reaches the plant.

By contrast, the inverted PET bottle delivers slow, continuous capillary moisture directly at root depth (20–40 mm below surface). Surface evaporation is cut to <5%, and runoff is zero, achieving empirical water savings of 65% to 73%.

📘 How this model works: Structural Safety Factor Formulation ▼

Load Equation: The total load on the top wall anchor is the cumulative wet mass of all \(N\) bottles:

Total Load = N · [ M_PET + (V_soil · ρ_soil_dry) + M_plant + (V_soil · Porosity · Saturation% · ρ_water) ]

Safety Factor: Standard structural engineering requires \(SF \ge 2.0\):

SF = min[ (BreakingStrength_rope · n_strands) / TotalLoad , HookRating / TotalLoad ]

Why Coco-Peat is Critical: Garden soil has a dry bulk density of 1.25 kg/L vs only 0.12 kg/L for coco-peat (10× difference). A 5-bottle stack filled with wet garden soil weighs ~14 kgf, snapping standard twine and loosening plastic wall plugs. Coco-peat keeps the total saturated load under 4.5 kgf, guaranteeing safe suspension.

📘 How this model works: Diurnal Cycles & Algae Prevention ▼

Diurnal Climate Modeling: Temperature oscillates sinusoidally between nighttime minima (e.g. 22°C at 5:00 AM) and afternoon heat peaks (38°C at 2:00 PM). Solar radiation drives transpiration peaks around midday.

Algae Formation in Clear PET: Transparent PET bottles allow photosynthetically active radiation to trigger green algae colonization. Algae clogs the 2mm orifice and competes for root oxygen. Painting the exterior opaque black eliminates solar light penetration, reducing algae colonization to 0%.

🎓 Viva-Prep: Mathematical Models, Principles & Limitations

1. Torricelli Outflow & Hydraulic Micro-Drip

Equation: Q = Cd · A · √(2gh) · (1 - Clogging) · F_backpressure · K_calib

Why used: Derives from Bernoulli's conservation of energy for fluid flowing through a small orifice under hydrostatic head height \(h\). \(C_d \approx 0.60\) accounts for flow contraction at sharp-edged drilled holes.

Physical Limitation: Standard Torricelli assumes free atmospheric discharge into open air. In our buried inverted bottle, wet soil at the cap interface creates a hydraulic counter-gradient and capillary boundary layer resistance, requiring calibration to match measured 20–80 ml/hr drip rates.

2. 2D Soil Moisture Finite-Difference Diffusion

Equation: ∂θ/∂t = D · (∂²θ/∂x² + ∂²θ/∂y²) - Evaporation - PlantRootUptake

Why used: Discretizes the cylindrical root zone into a 20×20 matrix to visualize capillary wicking away from the single drip emitter.

Why Coco-Peat: Coco-peat has a high diffusion coefficient (\(D=0.18\)) and massive field capacity (85% pore volume), spreading moisture laterally rather than letting it immediately channel straight down into runoff.

3. Structural Load & Factor of Safety (SF)

Equation: SF = min( (F_rope · n_strands) / TotalLoad , F_hook / TotalLoad )

Engineering Rule: Structural suspension requires \(SF \ge 2.0\). Water adds 1 kg per liter of pore volume. Garden soil saturated with water multiplies the hanging mass by nearly 3×, risking cord snap or wall hook pull-out. Lightweight coco-peat (0.12 kg/L dry density) is essential to maintain safety margins.

4. Project Management: Critical Path Method (CPM)

Concept: Identifies the longest dependency chain through the 6 micro-project activities. Tasks with zero total float (Total Float = LS - ES = 0) are critical. Testing (7 days) forms the principal bottleneck.

5. Monte Carlo Simulation (Triangular Distribution)

Equation: Samples durations between optimistic \(a\), likely \(m\), and pessimistic \(b\) over 1,000 iterations via inverse transform sampling to calculate probability of on-time delivery against college submission deadlines.
